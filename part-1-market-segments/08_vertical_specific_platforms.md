# Company type 3: Vertical-specific agent builder platforms

Vertical platforms win when they turn **domain knowledge + constraints + integrations + evaluation loops** into a defensible agent-building surface.

This section is intentionally *not* about “generic agent builders.” It’s about products where the buyer is already anchored to a domain outcome (support deflection, IT ticket resolution, AP close speed, hiring throughput, etc.) and the “agent builder” emerges as the configurable layer inside that domain.

---

## Segment definition (who they serve)

- Buyers anchored to a domain KPI (support deflection, IT ticket automation, AP close speed, contract turnaround, etc.)
- Teams that need deep integrations into domain systems‑of‑record and auditable execution

## What they build

- Domain‑specific agent/workflow builders (playbooks, skills, policies, guardrails)
- Deep connectors into vertical systems (CRM/helpdesk/CCaaS, ITSM, CLM, ERP, EHR, SOC tools)
- Evaluation and QA loops tied to domain KPIs (not generic “LLM accuracy”)

## What counts in this segment (validation bar)

A company belongs here if it satisfies **all** of the following:

1) **Domain-anchored outcome** (e.g., contact center automation, contract workflows, AP automation, healthcare ops).
2) **Configurable agent/workflow surface** exposed to the customer (builder, studio, playbooks, workflows, policies, skills, etc.).
3) **Integration depth** into domain systems-of-record (CRM/helpdesk/CCaaS, ITSM, CLM/DMS, ERP, EHR, SOC tools).
4) **Operational story** (monitoring, QA/evals, human-in-the-loop, auditability) tied to domain KPIs.

**Non-examples** (kept out on purpose):
- Generic “chat with your documents” wrappers.
- Pure infrastructure (SDKs, runtimes, browser automation) — those belong in Segment 1.
- Pure horizontal no-code builders — those belong in Segment 2.

- **vertical.legal.contracts** — contract review / CLM / legal workflow platforms with agentic workflows and playbooks.

## Segment hypotheses (updated, and actually falsifiable)

### V1 — Vertical agent platforms win by turning *domain KPIs* into eval harnesses

**Claim**: In verticals, “evals” are not a generic LLM accuracy problem — they are the business’s own metrics (AHT, deflection, first-contact resolution, AP cycle time, contract turnaround, etc.). Platforms that **instrument the KPI loop** become the default place to run agents.

**Why this matters**: This shifts defensibility from “our prompts are better” to “we own the measurement + feedback + operational workflow that defines success.”

**What would falsify it**: If horizontal builders (Segment 2) reliably match vertical platforms on *domain KPI deltas* with similar deployment effort.

---

### V2 — “AI employee” platforms are *vertical expansion engines* in disguise

**Claim**: The strongest “AI employee” companies start by automating **one role’s backlog** (support agent, AP clerk, recruiter, IT helpdesk) and then expand into adjacent workflows until they effectively become a mini-suite.

**Mechanism**:
- start with a narrow wedge where integration + correctness matters,
- earn write-access + trust,
- expand to neighboring workflows with the same identity, permissions, and context fabric.

**What would falsify it**: If these platforms cannot expand beyond the first role without heavy services or margins collapsing.

---

### V3 — No-code vs pro-code is not the core axis in high-stakes verticals; *control surface design* is

**Claim**: High-risk domains can still adopt UI-first systems *if* the product offers:
- deterministic guardrails,
- clear policy constraints,
- audit trails,
- and human checkpoints.

**Why it matters**: “Dev-first dominates high-risk” is too simplistic; what matters is whether the platform’s control surface maps to how the domain manages risk.

**Concrete example to sanity-check**: legal platforms (e.g., Harvey-style workflowization) can still be “UI-forward” and succeed if approvals + provenance are first-class.

**What would falsify it**: If regulated buyers consistently demand source-code-level control and reject UI-configurable systems even when governance is strong.

---

### V4 — Vertical platforms either become acquisition targets or must build suite-like adjacency

**Claim**: Standalone vertical platforms face a long-run squeeze from suites (Workday, ServiceNow, Salesforce, etc.). The two durable outcomes are:
1) **Become the system-of-record extension** (deeply embedded, hard to replace), or
2) **Get acquired** as suites internalize agent building + workflow automation.

**What would falsify it**: Multiple independent vertical platforms reaching very large scale without suite bundling pressure (or without being forced into adjacency expansion).

## Practical diligence prompts for this segment

When you talk to founders / buyers, the fastest “truth serum” questions:

1) **Where is the write-access?** Which systems do you mutate (not just read)?
2) **What is the domain KPI you move and how is it measured automatically?**
3) **What is the escape hatch?** Can power users drop to code / policies when the UI hits a wall?
4) **What is the deployment shape?** Self-serve, forward-deployed, or partner-heavy — and what does that do to margins?
5) **How do you expand?** Next workflow adjacent to the first one (and why is it unlocked by the same integrations)?
