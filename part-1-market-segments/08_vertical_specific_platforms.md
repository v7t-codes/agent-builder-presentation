# Company type 3: Vertical-specific agent builder platforms

Vertical platforms win when they turn **domain knowledge + constraints + integrations + evaluation loops** into a defensible agent-building surface.

This section is intentionally *not* about “generic agent builders.” It’s about products where the buyer is already anchored to a domain outcome — support deflection, IT ticket resolution, AP close speed, hiring throughput, and more — and the “agent builder” emerges as the configurable layer inside that domain.

---

## Segment definition

- They serve buyers anchored to a domain KPI: support deflection, IT ticket automation, AP close speed, contract turnaround, and similar.
- They serve teams that need deep integrations into domain systems-of-record and auditable execution.

## What they build

- They build domain-specific agent and workflow builders: playbooks, skills, policies, guardrails.
- They build deep connectors into vertical systems: CRM/helpdesk/CCaaS, ITSM, CLM, ERP, EHR, SOC tooling.
- They ship evaluation and QA loops tied to domain KPIs, not generic “LLM accuracy.”

## Validation bar

A company belongs here if it satisfies **all** of the following:

1) It is anchored to a domain outcome: contact center automation, contract workflows, AP automation, healthcare ops, and similar.
2) It exposes a configurable agent/workflow surface to the customer: builder, studio, playbooks, workflows, policies, skills, and similar.
3) It has integration depth into domain systems-of-record: CRM/helpdesk/CCaaS, ITSM, CLM/DMS, ERP, EHR, SOC tools.
4) It has an operational story tied to domain KPIs: monitoring, QA/evals, human-in-the-loop, auditability.

**Non-examples:** kept out on purpose.
- Generic “chat with your documents” wrappers do not belong in this segment.
- Pure infrastructure — SDKs, runtimes, browser automation — belongs in Segment 1.
- Pure horizontal no-code builders belong in Segment 2.

## Segment hypotheses

### Hypothesis 1 — **KPI loops** become the **moat** in vertical agent platforms

Vertical platforms win when they turn **domain KPIs** — AHT, deflection, first-contact resolution, AP cycle time, contract turnaround, and more — into an **eval + feedback loop** that drives improvement and **operational trust**.

- **Companies:** `Sierra` `Decagon` `Parloa` `Moveworks` `Serval` `Harvey`
- **Falsifiable test:** Horizontal builders in Segment 2 match KPI deltas with similar deployment effort and time-to-value.

---

### Hypothesis 2 — Vertical platforms reach **software-scale revenue** because they sell **outcomes**, not tools

Revenue potential is highest when the product is anchored to a **domain outcome** and ships an opinionated **“do the work” stack**: workflows + integrations + QA/evals, not a generic agent builder.

- **Companies:** `Serval` `Sierra` `Parloa` `Harvey` `Ironclad`
- **Falsifiable test:** Over time, these businesses look like services — low GM, linear scaling — rather than SaaS — high GM, compounding retention.

---

### Hypothesis 3 — Solving the **workflow search problem** creates **monopoly dynamics** and forces consolidation

In each vertical, the hardest early question is the **workflow search problem**: “which workflows do we automate first?” Teams that pick the right starting workflow — high volume, clear ROI, integration access — become the **default agent layer** for that domain and can compound toward **monopoly-like positions** via SI distribution, adjacency expansion, or suite acquisition.

- **Example:** `Serval` is a recent high-signal case: narrow ITSM workflows, e.g., access requests, and reported ~5× revenue growth in ~90 days, nearing ~$50M revenue run-rate.
- **Companies:** `Serval` `Moveworks` `Sierra` `Parloa`
- **Falsifiable test:** Verticals sustain multiple interchangeable vendors with low switching costs, and SIs treat platforms as commodities.
