# Company type 4: Agent studios & “Human+ software” companies

Agent studios are outcome‑driven teams that build and often operate agentic workflows inside a customer’s real environment. The buyer is paying for deployed behavior, not for a self‑serve builder product.

## Segment definition

- They serve enterprises with high-friction workflows where integration, governance, and on‑call reality matter.
- They serve teams that want to buy outcomes and accept a services plus operations engagement.

## What they build

- They build production agent systems with workflow design, tool/adaptor layers, permissions, and auditability.
- They run an operations layer with monitoring, eval/QA loops, incident response runbooks, and human escalation.
- They accumulate reusable internal tooling that is sometimes productized as a platform.

## Validation bar

A company belongs here if it satisfies **all** of:

1) It has an outcome/delivery-led GTM rather than being self-serve software first.  
2) It builds against real systems-of-record and can operate in production, or co‑operate with the customer.  
3) It has an explicit operating model with SLAs/runbooks, QA/evals, and human-in-the-loop where needed.

**Non‑examples:** kept out on purpose.

- Self‑serve no‑code builders belong in Segment 2.
- Framework and infrastructure providers belong in Segment 1.
- Domain platforms where the builder is the product belong in Segment 3.

## Subsegments

- **AI Systems Studios** — platform-assisted delivery of agentic systems in real enterprise environments
- **Human+ Operations Providers** — managed operations where humans-in-loop are part of the reliability contract

## Segment hypotheses

These hypotheses focus on the investable dynamics behind “agent studios” and “human+” operators without mixing in self-serve builder platforms.

### Hypothesis 1 — **Prototype-to-production** delivery factories outcompete pure strategy consultancies

Studios win when they can ship and operate **production behavior**: integrations, governance, on‑call, because the hard failure modes only appear in live workflows and operated systems.

- **Companies:** `a37` `FlowX.AI` `Invisible Technologies`
- **Falsifiable test:** Enterprise adoption shifts to self-serve internal teams with minimal partner involvement, and studios become optional rather than default.

---

### Hypothesis 2 — Durable studios quietly **become software companies**

Long-run winners convert repeated delivery into **reusable primitives**: connectors, eval harnesses, workflow skeletons, runbooks, and eventually a platform or platform-assisted delivery, so margins compound instead of scaling linearly with headcount.

- **Companies:** `a37` `Invisible Technologies`
- **Falsifiable test:** The best studios remain purely bespoke services yet still sustain high margins and category-leading growth without productization pressure.

---

### Hypothesis 3 — **Human+ ops** becomes the **operational moat** once agents hit production

Past the demo, the hard work is **incident response, drift, approvals, rollback, and continuous improvement**—so **human+ ops** becomes the moat in high-stakes workflows.

- **Companies:** `Invisible Technologies`
- **Falsifiable test:** Models and platform tooling improve enough that ongoing human operations becomes rare in production deployments.
